# Critique of "Seoul Planisphere" (astronomer seat) — WFF v4 feasibility lens

Reviewer stance: the engineer who has to ship this as declarative WFF v4 XML on an SM-R965N (Wear OS 6, 480x480, Samsung `com.samsung.wear.watchface.runtime`), no JavaScript. Every technical claim below was checked against developer.android.com/reference/wear-os/wff pages, the v4 XSDs in google/watchface, the memory-footprint evaluator source, the project's own validated `watchface.xml`, and a numeric script (`scratchpad/calc/astro_feas_check.py`, Inkscape raster of the official dragon in `scratchpad/calc/dragon/`).

**Score: 6 / 10.** Buildable and astronomically sound; as written it is still a star-map PNG under two baked gradients, it tells one lie about the sun, it specifies a transition WFF cannot author, and it gets four of its own technical facts wrong.

---

## 2. FATAL

### F1. Steady-state depth is two baked gradients; the stack is only legible during a transition the runtime owns
Occlusion is the proposal's depth argument ("three occlusion chains"), but nothing crosses an edge at a visible rate: the vault drifts 0.66 px/min at r150 (my check: 0.656 px/min, 944 px per day), the sun pointer 0.25 deg/min, the moon 8 px/day. In a glance, the only cues are the dome bevel (`#3E4F45` arc, 4 px wall, 12 px inner shadow) and the plate shadow (`#000 @60 %`, +3,+4, blur 8): a bevel ring and a drop shadow, both PNG. On a 40 mm OLED that is exactly the "skeuomorphic flat" look the user rejected. The only event that shows the stack is the wake choreography, and WFF hands its length and curve to the vendor (`duration` 0-1 "is the full extent allowed by the device"); with reduced motion, or if Samsung's runtime does not animate `scaleX`, the face is a star PNG under a ring.
**Fix (concrete):** move the seconds cage into the sky. Trail 1.7 px `#A6F46A @72 %` and 3.3 px marker + 6 px glow on a 292x292 carrier centred (225,225), radius 146, z-order between Z3 (moon) and Z4 (dome): the marker runs *under* the dome's 12 px inner shadow (r 138-150, so it visibly darkens as it passes the lit lower-right wall), goes *behind* the dragon's lower coil at the top (the raster shows 543 dragon pixels inside r150 around 11-1 o'clock, so the marker disappears for ~6-8 s each minute), and dips *under* the plate corners (plate corners at r 142-148, x 94/356, y 170/294). That is the Cosmotourbillon's job on the Galaxia (a bright object travelling in front of the sky, behind the instrument) at a rate the eye can see, with no loop and no extra per-second repaint beyond what v4 already does. F2 (r 192) keeps the 60 ticks, the sidereal pointer and the static AOD arcs.

### F2. "Below the horizon it is covered by the dome bitmap automatically" is false; the window hides the sun for most of a winter day
The window edge is altitude 6.1 deg due north, 23.9 deg due south, 25.6 deg east/west (verified). The true south horizon (dec -52.4) sits at y 423, outside the aperture (bottom y 375). Consequences at Seoul, apparent time:
- 25 Oct: sun above horizon 07:00-17:30, inside the window only 09:45-14:45.
- 21 Dec: above horizon 08:00-17:00, inside the window 11:15-13:45 (noon altitude 29.0 deg).
- 21 Mar: 06:45-18:30 vs 09:00-16:15. 21 Jun: 05:30-19:45 vs 07:15-18:00.
At 15:00 on a December afternoon the F1 day arc says daylight, the sun pointer is on the arc, and the sky in the window shows no sun and no haze. The owner will file it as a bug, and it breaks the proposal's own "everything on the face is true" thesis and the brief's "real astronomy".
**Fix A (recommended):** scale 1.6 px/deg, pole P = (225,120), zenith (225,204). Window bottom is then 255 px = 159 deg from the pole, so the southern horizon (r 228, y 348) is inside the aperture; north edge altitude becomes 9.6 deg. The horizon is a fixed curve in this projection (it depends only on latitude), so bake it into the dome PNG as a static ground band (`#0C110E` fill below the horizon curve, 10 px haze `#1A2420 -> 0` above it) and let the sun/moon slide under it. Big Dipper shrinks to 41 px, still legible. Re-run every coordinate in section 3/4 of the proposal (plate stays at y 170-294; the zenith then sits 1 px under the plate's top edge, which is fine).
**Fix B (if the 2 px/deg scale is kept):** state "sky above 25 deg" on the shoulder inscription, and let the F1 sun pointer carry a 3 px `#E2B15A` tick at r 150 on the aperture wall while the sun is hidden (`alt < 25` via the same expressions), so the sun is never "lost".

### F3. The transition as specified cannot be authored
WFF v4 has one `duration`/`startOffset`/`interpolation` per `Variant`, applied to the ambient state change; there is no per-direction timing and no absolute milliseconds. The "Active -> AOD, 180 ms, reverse order compressed" table is not expressible: the device plays the same fractions back (mirrored) on the way in, at its own length. `controls` gives a cubic-bezier, so `(.22,1,.36,1)` is fine. Also `Font` has no `Variant` child, so "fill #11200F -> #EFF8EC, stroke-opacity 1 -> 0" must stay a two-group crossfade (which the proposal already has for the digits; the text just claims more).
**Fix:** rewrite section 6 as fractions of one system transition, ordered back-to-front for wake, and accept the mirrored sleep. Example: vault crossfade `startOffset 0 duration .39`, dome `.11/.50` (+ scale), sun/moon `.17/.50`, plate+shadow `.22/.61`, digits `.22/.61`, orbit `.0/1.0` (angleDirection COUNTER_CLOCKWISE for the -14 deg settle), flange `.44/.56`, dragon `.56/.44`. Keep `startOffset + duration <= 1` everywhere ("startOffset is ignored" otherwise).

## 3. Required fixes (not fatal, must be done before a build)

1. **Outline digits exist in WFF.** `Font` accepts an `Outline` child (`color`, `width`, default 2.0, "distance the outline appears outside the edge"). Use `<Font family="roboto_mono" size="78" weight="SEMI_BOLD" color="#11200F"><Outline color="#91AD80" width="0.7"/></Font>` for the AOD clock. Drop the "Roboto Mono LIGHT" substitute and the claim in risk 9; the AOD lit % then matches the v4 measurement instead of needing a new one.
2. **Moon accuracy claim is wrong.** The proposal's `lamM = lamS + 12.1907*age + 6.289*sin(M')` is 2.7-2.9 deg off at both samples and up to 3.9 deg (8 px) over 2026-2027 against a 5-term Meeus-lite moon; at the real full moon (2026-10-26 04:12 UT) it gives elongation 176.9 deg. Add the evection and variation terms (`-1.274*sin(rad(M' - 2*Dm)) + 0.658*sin(rad(2*Dm))`, `Dm = 297.850 + 12.190749*D`) and use the mean longitude `218.316 + 13.176396*D` instead of the age chain; then the <= 1 deg claim is honest. Note the phase frame comes from the system (`MOON_PHASE_TYPE`) while the position comes from the expression; they can disagree by up to a day near quarters, which is acceptable but should be stated.
3. **Host `A_v` on an always-visible node.** `Reference.defaultValue` is "used while the parent element is not available such as when it is not visible". `vault_active` has alpha 0 in ambient, so any ambient consumer (`[REFERENCE.Av]` on arcs) would read the default. Put `Transform target="angle" value="A_v"` + `Reference name="Av" source="angle" defaultValue="0"` on a 1x1 transparent `PartDraw` that is never hidden, and reference that. Same for `raM`/`decM`/`LST` (host on `pivotX`/`x` of carriers, as the project does for `battery_sample`), otherwise the moon's ~700-character expression is copied 16 times across the 8 `Compare` branches.
4. **AOD may have at most two dynamic elements.** The project's gate runs the evaluator with `--apply-v1-offload-limitations` (2 layers, 2 clocks). The vault_aod (angle transform containing `HOUR_0_23_MINUTE`) is detected as an "old-style analog clock" and the `DigitalClock` is the second; the drifting AOD arcs (`Arc startAngle` transforms) and the ambient moon (`x`/`y` transforms) are not detected as clocks and are baked as static layers in that model. Make the four AOD arcs static at 12/102/192/282 deg (v4 geometry), show the AOD moon at its position at the moment of entering ambient only if the runtime proves it updates, and let the vault be the only thing that drifts in AOD. Verify on the SM-R965N with a 30-minute AOD soak (screencap is black in doze on this watch, so verify visually).
5. **Trail origin claim.** At A_v = 0, RA 0 h sits at 6 o'clock from P, while the trail starts at 12 o'clock of the dial; "where RA 0 h sits" is 180 deg off. Cleanest build: rotate the whole seconds `PartDraw` by `A_v + 180` (Transform `angle`, pivot .5/.5) and keep the arc at `startAngle 0`, `endAngle 6*[SECOND]`; that also avoids relying on `endAngle > 360` wrap behaviour, which is undocumented.
6. **Dragon numbers are wrong.** Rasterising the untouched official path at the proposed frame (135,14,180x123) gives ink x 145-305, y 13-132 (the proposal says x 166-285, y 47-126). The lowest ink is 6 px above Polaris's row, not 12; the pole itself is clear (nearest ink 52 px away, column above the pole clears at y 84). The wing tip at y 13 is 13 px from the dial clip and will touch it under AOD pixel shift. Move the frame to y 18 (ink y 17-136, still 2 px above Polaris's row, 48 px from the pole) or accept the raster and rewrite risk 5 as "no ink within 40 px of P".
7. **Sub-pixel budget.** Tier C (1.4 px), tier D (0.9 px @55 %), 0.5 px guilloche, 0.8 px constellation lines and 1 px rim lights will be resampled 450 -> 480 by the renderer unless the PNGs are baked at 546/480 px and the declared sizes stay 512/450; the proposal says so for bitmaps, but the studio renders at 450. Bake at 1.0667x, set the minimum stroke to 1.0 px *at 480*, and state the truth: ~720 catalogued, ~240 visible (A+B+C), tier D is texture.
8. **`dome_aod` as vectors.** A 450^2 bitmap whose content is one 1 px circle and 12 ticks costs 0.9 MB and, because its "everything else black" pixels are opaque, cannot be cropped by the optimizer. Draw it as `PartDraw` (`Ellipse` stroke `#3A4A3F` 1 px at r 150 + 12 `Line`s) with `alpha 0` -> `Variant AMBIENT 255`. Same for the F1 24 ticks/numerals if memory ever matters.
9. **Label the F1 ring `SOLAR`** (10 px `#5A6A5E`, TextCircular r 180, 150-170 deg); the proposal's own risk 7 says the owner will otherwise file the 49-minute offset as a bug.
10. **Plate over the zenith.** At 37.57 N the culmination band (dec 27-48: Vega, Deneb, Capella, the Dipper) is always under the glass. Use `#9e000000` (62 %) instead of 72 %, or shrink the plate to y 177-287 (110 px) so the band edges clear; keep the HH:MM baseline 252 untouched.
11. **Wording of precision.** Replace "exact to 0.01 deg" with "exact to the minute (<= 0.25 deg); float32-safe (<= 0.003 deg)".
12. **Studio parity.** The browser studio must render at 480 with the baked PNG layers, use one symmetric transition timeline, and show the AOD with only the vault moving; otherwise it previews four things the watch will not do (sub-pixel stars, asymmetric timing, drifting AOD arcs/moon, SVG-smooth 0.5 px lines).

## 4. Keep (genuinely strong)

- North-polar azimuthal-equidistant projection: the only projection where the whole sky is a rigid rotation about one pixel, i.e. one `Transform angle` on one bitmap. This is the single best decision in the proposal and it must not be traded away.
- The sidereal expression form: `DAY_OF_YEAR` + `HOUR_0_23_MINUTE` + `TIMEZONE_OFFSET_MINUTES_DST`, GMST epoch constant 100.660859 (verified exact), leap-year floor correct for 2024-2100, all operands < 1e5. Ship it as is (with the wording fix).
- Orientation and direction (east left, counter-clockwise), matching both the planisphere convention and the Galaxia's anticlockwise circuit.
- Apparent-solar sun model (declination, equation of time, 32.1-minute longitude correction) and the seasonal day arc on F1; positions reproduce.
- The z-order with measurable occlusion: dragon over the aperture rim (543 px of overlap), plate shadow onto the vault, dome inner shadow onto the sky.
- The bitmap/vector split: baked enamel vault and machined dome as PNG, everything that carries information (digits, arcs, ticks, inscriptions) as vector. Exactly what WFF wants; memory ~6 MB active / ~3 MB ambient.
- Two crossfaded `DigitalClock` groups at identical coordinates, HH:MM at 166/284 baseline 252 never moving (hard constraint honoured).
- Dropping `KALI / ORBIT` and the 27 px telemetry; the dragon brands, the plate carries one 14 px row.
- Palette discipline: one warm (`#E2B15A`), one cool neutral (`#C9D6D2`), enamel night `#04070A`; no blue, no purple.
- No loops, no twinkle, AOD under 15 %, drift-based burn-in safety.
- The `Reference` single-sourcing idea (already proven in this project's XML).

## 5. Answers to the four questions

1. **Cannot be expressed:** per-direction transition timing and absolute ms (F3); text fill/stroke animated by Variant on `Font` (use the crossfade); AOD elements beyond the vault + clock updating in offloaded ambient (fix 4); "endAngle > 360" wrap (unverified, avoid). Everything else maps to `PartImage/PartDraw/PartText + Transform + Variant + Condition + Reference`.
2. **Memory:** ~6.2 MB active, ~2.8 MB ambient by the evaluator's model (limits 100 / 10 MB). Not a risk. The real ambient constraint is the 2-clock/2-layer offload model, not bytes.
3. **Maths as expressions:** yes. GMST constant exact; LST within 0.25 deg (minute truncation) and float32-safe; sun model fine; moon needs two more terms to meet its own <= 2 deg claim (currently up to 3.9 deg).
4. **Studio over-promise:** sub-pixel strokes and 720 "stars", asymmetric WAAPI timing, drifting AOD arcs/moon, 450-px rendering of a 480-px panel, and the claim that outline digits are impossible on the watch (they are not; `Outline` exists). Fix the studio to the watch, not the other way round.

## Verdict
Astronomically correct, WFF-portable, comfortably inside memory, and the right projection. But the depth the user asked for lives only in two baked gradients and one vendor-controlled transition, the sun lie will be noticed in the first winter week, and the transition spec is written for a browser. Put the seconds cage in the sky (F1), show the horizon or label the window (F2), rewrite the motion as one symmetric fractional timeline (F3), and correct the four false claims (Outline, moon, dragon bbox, precision). Then it is a 8-9.
