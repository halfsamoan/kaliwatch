# Critique of the "material" proposal (KALIWATCH v5 Celestial Vault) — readability / AOD / burn-in / battery lens

Reviewer stance: Wear OS reviewer, adversarial. Score **6 / 10**. Verdict: **strong instrument, wrong exposure.** The stack is real and buildable, but as specified the one layer that is supposed to make it "not flat" (z4 `dome_light`) is baked onto a ground so dark that it cannot be seen, the AOD burn-in walk moves HH:MM, and the WFF mapping leans on a Scene-level `<Expressions>` that does not exist. Fix those three and this is an 8-9.

Everything below was checked, not assumed. Measurements come from a 2x raster of the proposal's own §5 AOD spec (PIL + numpy, RobotoMono.ttf from the repo, the official dragon path from `dragon-data.js`, scaled 1.143 into box x153 y30), averaged to 450 px and counted inside the r225 disc (159,068 px). Script: `scratchpad/debate/tools/aod_lit_material.py`; render: `scratchpad/debate/tools/aod-material-mock.png`. WFF claims were checked against developer.android.com reference pages on 2026-10-25.

---

## Answers to the five mandatory questions

**1. Can HH:MM be read in under one second at 40 mm with that sky behind it?**
Yes, with conditions. The glyph strokes are ~9 px (9.6 px on the 480 panel, ~0.8 mm) in `#EFF8EC` (91.5% linear luminance). Even at the proposal's glaze peak the dome under the digits computes to (29,35,40) = L* 13 → contrast ratio ≈ 14:1. If the ground is lifted as I demand in F1 (lit side L* 25-30), the ratio at the worst point is still ≈ 8:1. What threatens the sub-second read is not the dome, it is clutter at the glyph edges: the catalogue is ~385 stars to m 4.0 at dec > -30 (the proposal says ~200; see T3), 18 line figures, 7 px constellation names and the Milky Way all pass under the digits because the digit box (x119-331, y195-252) lies entirely inside r110 of the pole. Required: no constellation names inside r130, star alpha cap 0.5 (not 0.6) inside r120, no core above 1.8 px inside r120, central well alpha 0.5. With those, yes, < 1 s.

**2. Any text under ~11 px treated as information?**
One case. The 9 px zodiac glyphs at alpha .7 on a 1 px dashed ring are described as "the month/zodiac reading". At 40 mm a 9 px glyph is 0.75 mm: nobody reads a sign from it; what the user actually sees is a gold dot on a dashed ring. Acceptable as texture, but the document must stop calling it a reading. Constellation names (7 px), trench dots (1 px), tick grooves (1 / 0.6 px) are correctly declared texture. BATTERY / STEPS labels at 12 px sit 1 px above the brief's threshold and are labels for 24 px values, so they pass. INFINITY at 11 px in AOD is decoration, fine.

**3. AOD under 15 percent lit with no per-second motion?**
Measured **8.62%** of the disc lit (any channel > 0), **5.40%** if the `#11200F` glyph fill is excluded; mean linear luminance over the disc 1.5% of full white (the honest power proxy). Under the 15% limit, so the constraint holds, but the proposal's "~7,700 px = 4.8%" is wrong because it omits the 5,117 px of `#11200F` digit fill (v4's 11% figure counted that fill) and undercounts stars (2,741 px with halos for ~130 stars, not 360). Per-layer new lit px from the raster: stars 2,741; horizon hairline 737; flange glint + indices 1,692; bead + moon 314; dragon contour 1,208; digit fill 5,117; digit stroke 593; date/BAT/INFINITY 1,303. No per-second element survives in AOD (seconds beads, arc, telemetry all alpha 0). The only AOD motion is the 0.25 deg/min sidereal turn (1 px/min at the disc edge) and the per-minute walk: compliant.

**4. Burn-in from static bright rings?**
Rings are fine: glint arc `#55605E` = 11.1% linear luminance, 2 px; indices at 40% of `#B9C4C0` = 7.5%; horizon hairline ≈ 2%; dragon contour `#5E7A52` = 16.9%, 1.2 px. The failure is the proposal's own sentence "nothing static above 22% luminance": the AOD digit outline `#91AD80` is **37.5%** and the date `#7F9472` is **26.9%** linear luminance, and 5,573 px of the AOD frame sit at or above 20%. The minute glyphs move every minute and the hour glyphs every hour, so they are tolerable; the **colon** is the one glyph that is static all night at 37.5%, and the date is static for 24 h. See fix R4.

**5. Anything that loops or twinkles?**
No twinkle: stars are baked into the disc and only rotate. No decorative loop in Active: the comet tail is four fixed-offset groups driven by `[SECOND]` (stepped), the arc end creeps 0.1 deg/s. In AOD the "pseudo-random" walk is a 5-minute loop: for `[MINUTE]` 0..4 the offsets are (-2,-2), (0,1), (2,-1), (-1,2), (1,0) and then repeat, because `7m % 5` and `3m % 5` both have period 5. Sub-perceptual, so not a violation, but it is not random and it only ever visits five positions, which is a weak burn-in measure (each pixel dwells 1/5 of the night in each of five spots). See F2.

## FATAL (as specified)

**F1. The dome is invisible: the shading is baked onto a near-black ground, so the face is still "stars plus rings".**
Computed from §1.1 ground + §1.2 glaze/terminator: glaze peak (168,150) → (30,35,39) L* 13.2; pole → (26,32,38) L* 11.9; lit rim (100,100) → (18,21,24) L* 6.6; dark rim (350,350) → (12,17,23) L* 4.9. The entire "dome" lives in a 20-code-value band between L* 5 and L* 13. On a Samsung AMOLED that band is where black-level crush and low-level banding live; indoors it reads as a faint vignette, outdoors (glass reflection ≈ 1% of 10,000 lux ≈ 30 nits versus a dome peak of ≈ 10-15 nits at 500 nits full white) it vanishes completely. The proposal's own risk #1 admits this and offers a 1.3x alpha bake, which moves the peak to L* 15. The user's complaint was flatness; this ships flat.
Fix: raise the grisaille ground so the lit half of the dome sits at L* 22-30 (ground `#2A3440` at the glaze side, `#1C242C` mid, `#0E1318` far side), glaze alpha .22 (ease-out r150) plus a small specular hot-spot r25 at (168,150) alpha .35, terminator to black alpha .60 at r196, keep the 2% grain and add 1-bit dither to the bake so the 8-bit gradient does not band, and raise the central well to alpha .50 so the digits keep ≥ 8:1. Verify on the device at 3 brightness levels before any other polish; this is the one on-device test that decides the project.

**F2. The AOD burn-in walk moves HH:MM.** The ambient-only Group with `x = (([MINUTE]*7) % 5) - 2`, `y = (([MINUTE]*3) % 5) - 2` contains the outline digits, so (a) the digits jump up to 4 px every minute in AOD and (b) at wake the outline digits are up to 2.8 px off the filled `DigitalClock` glyphs during the 140-320 ms crossfade, producing a visible double image. "HH:MM never moves" is a hard constraint.
Fix: digits, colon and date leave the walk group. Apply the walk only to dragon contour, glint arc, indices, horizon hairline and INFINITY, or drop it entirely (five positions do little; the thin strokes and R4's colour cap do more).

**F3. "Declare once as `<Expressions>` on the Scene" cannot be built.** The `Scene` reference lists its allowed children as Group, Condition, ListConfiguration, BooleanConfiguration, Variant, ComplicationSlot, AnalogClock, DigitalClock, Transform, Reference; `WatchFace` allows Scene, BitmapFonts, Metadata, UserConfigurations. Named `Expression`s exist only inside a `Condition`, their names are unique within that Condition, and only `Compare expression="name"` may reference them; a `Transform value` is a raw arithmetic expression. So LST, lam, RAsun, decSun, thSun, age, lamMoon, RAmoon, decMoon, thMoon must be inlined into every Transform that uses them. The true-sun x/y and moon x/y expand to roughly 400-700 characters each; written per sprite as proposed (8 moon sprites x 2 axes) that is ~11 kB of hand-maintained expression text.
Fix: generate the XML from the studio (template + the JS astronomy functions emitting strings), never by hand; write each formula once by putting the `Transform x/y` on a positioning `Group` and the 8-way `Condition` on `[MOON_PHASE_TYPE]` inside that Group (Group may contain Condition: verified); same for the true sun; run the official WFF validator and `memory-footprint` on the output.

## Required FIXES (non-fatal, must land)

**R1. Bake at device resolution.** All PNGs are specified at 450 and the face is 450 wide; the 480 px panel forces a 1.0667x resample ("Resizing to better fit the screen" is explicitly part of the memory pipeline). Every 1 px groove, 0.6 px lit edge, 0.8 px constellation line and 1 px dashed ecliptic becomes a 2 px soft line. Declare `<WatchFace width="480" height="480">` and export every bitmap at 480 (coordinates x 1.0667), or export at 960 and let the renderer downsample. Memory stays trivial (sky 480: 0.9 MB RGBA; ambient ALPHA_8: 0.23 MB) against the verified limits of 10 MB ambient / 100 MB interactive.

**R2. The horizon and ecliptic must be the true curves, not circles.** The "circle centred (225,78) r208 is within 2 px" claim is false: against the exact Seoul horizon (phi 37.57 N, azimuthal-equidistant, s = 1.633) the circle is off by up to **21 px** on-disc (az 128 deg, near (401,141)). The "off-centre circle" ecliptic is off by **5.3 px** at the equinoxes, so the true-sun sprite would float 5 px off the zodiac ring every March and September, exactly when the proposal says it "crosses the ring". The proposal says the bake uses the exact curve; make sure the studio code does too and delete the circle shortcuts from the document.

**R3. Sidereal expression: split the day fraction out of D.** If WFF evaluates in float32 (the arithmetic-expression page says only "floating-point" and links java.lang.Math; precision is undocumented), D = 9793.5475 has an ulp of 84 s, so `360*fract(D)` steps LST by 0.35 deg (1.2 px at the disc edge) every 84 s. Cost-free restructure: `Dint = 365*([YEAR]-2000) + floor(([YEAR]-1)/4) - 499 + [DAY_OF_YEAR] - 1`, `f = ([SECONDS_IN_DAY] - 60*[TIMEZONE_OFFSET_MINUTES_DST])/86400 - 0.5`, `LST = (280.46061837 + 0.98564736629*Dint + 360.98564736629*f + 126.978 + 720) % 360`. Negative f before 09:00 local is fine. Verified the proposal's own check date: D = 9793.5475, LST 177.5 deg, matches. (`[TIMEZONE_OFFSET_MINUTES_DST]` is version 3+: state the minimum WFF version.) Also quantise the astronomical layers to the minute (`floor([SECONDS_IN_DAY]/60)*60`) so the 418 px sky blit, sun bead and sprites do not re-evaluate every second in interactive mode; only the vector bead and arc need the per-second tick.

**R4. Static AOD luminance cap, honestly applied.** Keep `#91AD80` for the changing glyphs; set the colon and the date to `#6E8862` (21.8% linear luminance) so nothing static exceeds the proposal's own 22% rule. Rewrite the §5 sentence and the lit-pixel paragraph with the measured numbers (8.6% / 5.4% / 1.5% mean luminance) and keep the `#11200F` fill (it hides stars inside the counters) but count it.

**R5. Sun bead goes under `dome_light`, not over it.** As ordered (z5 above z4) the bead's r8 halo lies on top of the trench AO and spills over the dome rim at r196 onto the sky, so it reads as a sticker on the rim, not an object down in the channel; the flange only clips the outer half. Draw the bead below `dome_light` so the U-profile AO (0.72 → 0.30 → 0.72) shades the halo toward both walls. The bead stays visible through the 0.30 floor alpha.

**R6. Replace the hard-offset digit shadow.** A +2,+3 black offset at alpha .6 with no blur is invisible against a dark ground and only appears where it overlaps a star or the Milky Way, where it reads as misregistration, not as an air gap. What actually protects the glyph edges against a rotating star field is a 1-2 px dark surround: four extra `TimeText` copies at (±1,0),(0,±1) in `#000000` alpha .55 under the white one (DigitalClock has no stroke). Cheap, static, and it does the readability job the shadow claims to do.

**R7. TimeText formats.** `format="HH"` is not in the documented set (examples are `hh:mm:ss`, `h:mm`, `hh_10`, `hh_1`, `m`, `mm_10`, `mm_1`, `ss`, `ss_10`, `ss_1`). Use `hh` + `hourFormat="24"` as the existing watchface.xml does, or the documented per-digit tokens `hh_10 hh_1 mm_10 mm_1`, which also make the bitmap-font ambient glyphs trivially aligned per character.

**R8. BitmapFont crossfade alignment.** `BitmapFont family/size/color` inside `TimeText` is valid (verified) and the glyph set 0-9 plus colon is fine, but vector `TimeText` places glyphs by box + align + baseline while `Character width/height` places bitmaps by box; a 1 px mismatch becomes a visible ghost during the 140-300 ms crossfade. Pad the glyphs to 52 x 64 so the 1.4 px stroke is not clipped, and make pixel-exact alignment an on-device acceptance test.

**R9. Star counts and clutter.** Whole-sky counts are 171 stars to m 3.0 and 513 to m 4.0; dec > -30 is 75% of the sky, so the disc holds ~128 and ~385 stars, not ~90 and ~200. Double the planned density means the r130 alpha cap and the 1.4 px minimum core matter twice as much, and the AOD star layer is ~2,700 px not 360. Either cut Active to m 3.5 (~260) or accept and re-run the readability check with the autumn-evening Cassiopeia/Cepheus sky under the digits, which the proposal itself names as the worst case.

**R10. Variant timing hygiene.** Verified rule: if `startOffset + duration > 1.0` the startOffset is silently ignored. The proposal's pairs all sum to ≤ 1.0 (digit shadow .61 + .39 and arc .56 + .44 are exactly 1.0), so keep them there, and use the documented `angleDirection` attribute on the -6 → 0 flange and -14 → 0 arc Variants so the renderer cannot pick the long way round.

## KEEP (genuinely strong)

- **Polaris under the colon with radial-only legibility caps.** Because the star-alpha cap (r130), the Milky Way cap (r110) and the central well are all functions of radius, the protection is rotation-invariant: the busiest sky can rotate under the digits and the guarantee holds. This is the correct engineering answer to a rotating background, keep it exactly.
- **Digits as the top, immovable plane.** HH:MM at x166/x225/x284, baseline 252, never scaled, with everything else choreographed around it. The 360 ms order (sky → light → flange → sprites → dragon → digits → shadow → arc → telemetry) reveals depth back-to-front and keeps the v4 signatures (-14 deg arc, 80 ms telemetry delay).
- **Three physical planes that each carry a different real time scale**: sky disc (sidereal), trench bead (24 h solar + zodiac), flange groove arc/bead (minutes/seconds), glass (civil). That is the brief's definition of "not one-dimensional", satisfied on paper. The minute arc and seconds bead sitting exactly on the engraved tick radius r208 is the detail that keeps the vector parts from floating.
- **Comet tail as four fixed-offset groups** driven by `[SECOND]*6 - k*6`: zero animation cost, no loop, stepped.
- **AOD as an engraved line drawing**: ALPHA_8 bitmaps + `tintColor` (attribute verified on PartImage and Group), contour dragon, outline digits, m ≤ 3.0 stars, one 2 px glint arc. Same silhouettes as Active, 8.6% lit, 1.5% mean luminance, no per-second motion. The horizon haze giving the telemetry band a physical reason (it is the Earth) is a smart composition move.
- **One key light for every bake (315 deg, 35 deg elevation)** and source-over compositing only: no blend modes, so no WFF version gating.
- **WFF feasibility of the maths.** `fract floor asin sin cos rad deg clamp` and `%` all exist; `YEAR`, `DAY_OF_YEAR`, `SECONDS_IN_DAY`, `TIMEZONE_OFFSET_MINUTES_DST`, `MOON_PHASE_POSITION` (0-28), `MOON_PHASE_TYPE` (0-7), `BATTERY_PERCENT`, `STEP_COUNT` all exist with the ranges assumed; Arc `endAngle` is transformable; Group/PartImage `angle alpha scaleX scaleY` are transformable so every Variant target used is legal; memory (~4 MB / ~1.5 MB) is far inside the verified 100 MB / 10 MB limits.
- **The honest risk list** (bezel overlap, resampling, latitude dependence, moon error ~6 deg, device-timed Variants). Keep it, and append F1-F3 and R1-R10 to it.
