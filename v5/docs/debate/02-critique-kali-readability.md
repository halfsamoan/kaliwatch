# Critique 02 — proposal 01 "Celestial Vault" (lens: kali)

Reviewer role: Wear OS reviewer for readability, AOD lit-pixel budget, burn-in and battery. Adversarial by instruction.
Design space 450x450, centre (225,225), r 225; device Galaxy Watch6 Classic 480x480 (x1.0667, 40 mm).

## Verdict

**Score 4 / 10.** Right architecture, wrong physics. The layer stack is the best structure proposed so far and should be kept, but as specified it ships (1) a mirror-image sky rotating the wrong way, (2) "materials" whose entire tonal range is 0.3-3.5 % relative luminance, so every occlusion shadow, bevel and chiaroscuro the depth depends on is below what an OLED shows on a wrist, (3) a reticle that spins forever at 6 deg/s, and (4) a dragon that, at the stated frame, lies across the hour ring and the outer flange and occults the sun pointer for about two hours every late morning. Fix those four and this becomes the base to build on.

## Answers to the five mandatory questions

1. **HH:MM read in under one second at 40 mm with that sky behind it?** Active: yes. 78 px Roboto Mono 600 at `#EFF8EC` (relative luminance 0.915) inside a 55 % glass slot over a sky whose brightest baked element is a 4.7 px `#B8C4BE` dot at 80 % (0.534 x 0.8 x 0.45 glass = about 0.19 luminance under the slot before alpha blending; worst case Saturn's 11 px lit limb, about 0.05 after the glass). Contrast stays above 9:1 everywhere under the slot; the proposal's "12:1" is optimistic but the read is safe. The real threat is the AOD read: 1.4 px outline strokes (1.5 px at 480) at luminance 0.375 around a 0.012-luminance fill. That is the thinnest information-carrying element on the face and it is the one the user looks at most. Not instant; see fix F8.
2. **Any text under ~11 px treated as information?** Yes, twice. The hour ring's 00/06/12/18 numerals are 9 px (0.8 mm) and are the scale the sun pointer is read against; the zodiac band glyphs are 9 px at 35 % grisaille (an sRGB value around `#404442`, invisible on a wrist). Fix F9.
3. **AOD under 15 % lit with no per-second motion?** Yes. The component arithmetic is sound (my own count of the dragon hairline is lower than the proposal's: the flattened path perimeter is about 1,190 px in 450 space, so 1.2 px plus AA is about 1,400-2,400 lit px, not 3,000). Total about 10-11.5 k px = 6.5-7.2 % of 159,043; luminance-weighted well under 3 %. Nothing in AOD is driven by `[SECOND]`; ambient renders once a minute (Google design guidance: "the screen is only updated once every minute"). The vault/sentinel/ember per-minute drift is allowed slow drift.
4. **Burn-in from static bright rings?** No bright static rings: `hour_ring_aod` indexes `#3F5A43` (luminance 0.088) and `flange_aod` ticks `#2E4A33` (0.057) are static but dim hairlines. The only static element above luminance 0.3 is the outline digits (`#91AD80`, 0.375) in fixed glyph boxes, which the "HH:MM never moves" constraint forces. Acceptable only with an explicit jitter (fix F10); "the system's periodic AOD pixel shift" is asserted, not verified, for WFF faces and must not be relied on.
5. **Anything that loops or twinkles?** Yes. (a) The sentinel cage spins 360 deg per minute, forever, in Active, with a 3-fold-symmetric glyph, so it is a 20-second visual loop that carries no information the trail and `SEC nn` do not already carry: a decorative loop at perceptible speed, which the brief forbids. (b) A hidden twinkle: rotating a bitmap of 1.0-1.2 px star dots by 0.25 deg steps once a minute re-samples every dot at a new sub-pixel phase; a 1.2 px dot's peak intensity varies up to 2x between phases, so the whole field flickers once a minute. The proposal says "no twinkle" and then builds one.

## FATAL

**F1. The sky is a mirror image and rotates the wrong way (breaks "real astronomy").**
Derivation, independent of any chart convention: facing north, stars circle Polaris counter-clockwise (hour angle H increases CCW; classic star-trail fact). RA = LST - H, so at a fixed instant RA increases in the opposite sense: **clockwise** around the pole on any correct inside-view chart. Check with the Plough at upper culmination (LST about 12.4 h): Dubhe (RA 11.06 h) has H = +1.3 h (west, already culminated), Alkaid (RA 13.8 h) has H = -1.4 h (east); facing north east is on the right, so the handle (higher RA) is on the right at the top of the circle, i.e. RA increases clockwise ("arc to Arcturus" in the east confirms it). The proposal bakes RA increasing counter-clockwise and turns the vault clockwise, which is the god's-eye (celestial globe) view: every constellation is flipped and the sky runs backwards. The spot-check table is therefore the mirrored table (e.g. Vega baked at (319.8, 209.6); in a correct bake it is at (130.2, 209.6)). The JLC reference turns anticlockwise ("circuiting anti-clockwise along with the rest of the main dial structure ... contrary to the hands"), which is the correct sense. The author half-saw this (risk 2). It is not a risk, it is the current state.
Fix (one sign flip plus a mirrored bake, as the author said): bake with x = 225 + r sin(RA), y = 225 - r cos(RA), RA measured clockwise from 12 o'clock; rotate the vault with `Transform target="angle" value="360 - LST_DEG"` (WFF angles are clockwise-positive); east is then at 3 o'clock, west at 9; the horizon bitmap is symmetric and needs no change; the sun pointer must also run counter-clockwise (`angle = 0 - SUN_HA`, 06 index at 3 o'clock, 18 at 9 o'clock) because the sun is a sky object and its hour angle must agree with the vault. Verify on device against Stellarium at wake: Vega at the sample time (LST 11h50) must sit at about (131, 245), lower left, altitude about 15 deg.

**F2. The depth cannot be seen: every "material" lives between 0.3 % and 3.5 % relative luminance (will still read as flat).**
Measured from the proposal's own hexes (sRGB linearised, Rec. 709 weights): vault base `#06100C` 0.4 % -> `#0B1A14` 0.9 %; hour-ring floor `#163A3C` 3.5 % -> `#0A1C1D` 1.0 %; flange `#0E1A12` 0.9 % -> `#050A06` 0.3 %; earth (78 % black over the vault) 0.2 %; glass (55 % black over the vault) 0.4 %. Consequently the ring's "65 % occlusion shadow" is a 0.9 % -> 0.3 % step, the horizon chiaroscuro is 0.9 % vs 0.2 %, the slot is 0.9 % vs 0.4 %, and the dragon's 60 % cast shadow falls on a 0.9 % surface. At a typical indoor 150-200 nits full-white, a 0.9 % surface is about 1.5 nits and its shadow 0.5 nits; glass reflections in a lit room are several nits. Outdoors at 1,000+ nits ambient the entire relief is black. The only depth cue that survives is overlap of lit objects, and the proposal uses exactly one (dragon apex over the ring). Section 8 ("each plane throws a shadow on the one beneath it") describes the spec, not the pixels. The reference itself does not do this: the Galaxia's dome is midnight-blue grisaille enamel, a mid-tone surface with white painted on it, which is why its shadows read.
Fix: give the planes a luminance ladder that an OLED can show, while keeping the void black where it matters. Proposed: sky (vault body above the horizon) lit region `#3A4A42` (6.2 %) at the 10 o'clock side of the dome falling to `#141C18` (1.0 %) at the 4 o'clock side, so the vault reads as a lit dome (the Galaxia "domed celestial disc" cue, and it is bakeable); ring floor keep `#163A3C` (3.5 %) with its shadow band at 1 %; flange face about 1.5 % with a 3 px lip highlight `#45574B` (8.6 %) at the 10-11 o'clock arc only; earth 100 % black (`#000000`, 12-16 px feather at the horizon), so the telemetry sits on true black and the horizon finally exists as a shape, not a hairline; glass 55 % black over the 6 % sky gives about 2.7 % behind the digits: digit contrast (0.915+0.05)/(0.027+0.05) = 12.5:1, still excellent. The dragon's 60 % shadow on a 6 % sky is a 3.6-point step: visible for the first time. AOD is untouched by this (the AOD planes are darkening-only plus hairlines), so the budget does not move. Also: a 5-unit sRGB gradient over 186 px (`#06100C` -> `#0B1A14`) is five discrete bands; the ladder above removes that banding as a side effect.

**F3. The sentinel cage is a perpetual decorative loop (breaks a hard constraint).**
360 deg per minute at 6 deg/s with a 3-fold glyph = 20 s period, forever, in Active. The seconds are already shown by the trail, the marker and `SEC nn`. "Like a tourbillon cage" is a metaphor, not information. The brief: "no infinite decorative loops at perceptible speed". Fix: delete the spin; the sentinel remains a clock by its sidereal orbit (and gains meaning if it is actually placed on the vernal point, see F6). If a per-second element is wanted at that spot, it must replace the trail, not duplicate it.

**F4. The dragon at frame (135,28,180x123) lies across both rings and occults the sun pointer for about two hours a day; the stated "apex r197 at x225" is false.**
I flattened the unchanged press-pack path (467 samples) in the proposal's frame: the outermost point is (143.6, 28.3) at r 212.9, inside the outer flange (r206-225); 38 samples lie beyond r190, spanning 333.0-359.4 deg clockwise from 12. Near x225 the top of the path is y 32.1 (r 193). Consequences at the proposal's z-order (dragon z9 above rings z6/z8): the "11" hour index (345 deg) is permanently hidden and "10" clipped; minute ticks 56-59 are hidden; the seconds marker (r216, z8) vanishes behind the dragon for seconds 56-59 every minute; the sun glyph (r189-207, about 5 deg wide) is hidden from about 331 to 2 deg of hour angle, i.e. apparent solar 10:04-12:08 (about 10:20-12:24 KST in late October), the part of the day the user looks at the watch most. "Noon eclipse, about 40 minutes, a feature" is wrong by 3x and is not a feature. Fix, pick one: (a) keep the frame and put the dragon **below** the ring stack (z between the glass and the hour ring): the wing tip tucks under the sunken ring's lip, which is the single strongest and cheapest occlusion cue available, and it is exactly the Galaxia logic (objects under rims); the path is untouched, only 5-8 % of its outline is occluded; sun and seconds then pass in front; or (b) shrink to 140x96 at (155,50): the extreme point moves to (161.7, 50.2), r 185.9, bottom y 146 clear of the date; everything stays visible, at the cost of 22 % dragon size. I recommend (a).

## Required fixes (non-fatal, must be done before a build)

- **F5. WFF trig is radians.** `sin/cos/asin/acos/atan` link to Java `Math` and the inverse ranges are given in pi; `deg()`/`rad()` exist. Every formula in section 6 is in degrees: wrap as `sin(rad(2*B))`, `cos(rad(B))`. `[SECONDS_SINCE_EPOCH]` exists but is WFF **version 3**; `[HOUR_0_23_MINUTE]` and `[TIMEZONE_OFFSET_MINUTES_DST]` exist (v1/v3). The v3 requirement is fine for the project (the existing XML already uses `interpolation` on Variant, which is v4), but the fallback is `[UTC_TIMESTAMP]/1000`; verify `%` on that magnitude on device. `[MONTH_S]` and `[DAY_OF_WEEK_S]` are locale strings: on a ko-KR watch they render "10월" and "일", not "OCT / SUN". Build the date line from `Condition` on `[MONTH]` (12 literals) and `[DAY_OF_WEEK]` (7 literals), or use numerals as the existing XML does. `Compare expression` must reference a named boolean `Expression` (as the existing `cursor_on` does): define `mp0..mp7`, do not inline `mp == n`. Zero-padding the LST minutes needs a Condition (`numberFormat` is documented with `#`, `,`, `.` only).
- **F6. Astronomy consistency.** The vernal point (RA 0h, dec 0) is at r 168.75, not r 178; put the sentinel there or stop calling it the vernal point. Sirius (r 200) is under the opaque hour ring and is never visible: delete the row. "Milky Way wash never under the slot" is impossible for a layer that rotates under a fixed slot: delete the claim. The sentinel sits above the horizon layer (z4 > z2) and keeps glowing below the horizon: put it below the horizon layer so it sets and rises with the sky (with F2's opaque earth it contributes 0 lit px half the day). Directional shading baked into rotating layers contradicts the one scene light: the sun glyph's baked shadow (dx 1.5, dy 2.5) points toward 10 o'clock when the sun is at 6 o'clock, and Saturn's "lit limb" turns with the vault. Remove baked shadows from rotating glyphs (keep the glow); shade painted planets with a central highlight, or nest a counter-rotated child (`angle = 0 - SUN_HA`) if the shadow must stay.
- **F7. Shadows that cancel each other (sunken ring reads as raised).** The hour ring gets a uniform inner shadow all round plus a 1.5 px grisaille highlight on its top-left outer edge. Under a 10 o'clock light, a stepped bowl (flange > ring > vault > slot) has its shadows on the **top-left** inside of each step and lit lips on the bottom-right; a top-left highlight is the signature of a convex ring. The glass slot gets this right ("top wall in shade"); the rings do not. Bake: vault rim shadow r182-190 strongest at 10 o'clock fading to 0 by 4 o'clock, with a 1.5 px lit lip at 2-6 o'clock; ring floor outer shadow r198-206 on the 8-12 o'clock arc; flange lip highlight 8-12 o'clock only. Bake at 480 (or 2x) as the author already notes; 1.5 px bevels authored at 450 will not survive the 1.0667 scale.
- **F8. AOD digit stroke.** Use `Font > Outline` (Wear OS 4, `width` default 2.0): fill `#11200F`, outline `#91AD80`, width 1.8-2.0, not 1.4. That is about +1,000 lit px (still under 8 %) and turns the AOD read from "possible" to "instant". The 11-glyph BitmapFont in the asset list is unnecessary.
- **F9. Sub-11 px text.** Replace the 9 px hour-ring numerals with long ticks at 00/06/12/18 and state that the sun pointer is read against the 12/6/3/9 positions; or make the four majors 12 px. Delete the 9 px zodiac band (invisible, and it rotates under the STEPS value). One zodiac display (the 24 px glyph) is enough; the date line's "SCO" and the band make three.
- **F10. Burn-in mitigation must be the default, not risk 10.** Add `Transform target="x" value="127 + (floor([MINUTE]/10) % 3) - 1"` (and the same on y with a different phase) to the AOD DigitalClock, colon and BAT line: a 1 px walk every 10 minutes. Cap every static AOD element at luminance <= 0.38 (the outline is the ceiling). Do not cite "the system's periodic AOD pixel shift" until it is observed on an SM-R965N.
- **F11. Star-field shimmer under rotation.** Minimum baked dot 2.0 px with a 0.6 px soft edge (no 1.0-1.2 px dots); AOD stars 2.5 px soft. Verify on device whether the WFF renderer filters rotated bitmaps; if it does not, bake the vault at 2x and let the scaler filter.
- **F12. Layer count and the "lift".** Horizon, channel and hour ring are all static; merge them into one full-disc PNG (the 0.985 -> 1.0 "lift" is a 3 px effect nobody will see, and as specified the `hour_ring_aod` sibling is not scaled, so the two rings are 3 px apart during the crossfade). That leaves two full-disc layers in Active (static stack + rotating vault) plus the flange, halving per-frame compositing and about 2 MB of memory. Memory as proposed is about 8.3 MB active at 480 (eight full-disc RGBA8888 bitmaps at 921.6 KB each plus small parts) against the 100 MB limit, and about 2.8 MB ambient (the evaluator counts at most three full-screen ambient layers) against 10 MB: fine either way, but the merge is free.
- **F13. Text outside the glass after F2.** Once the sky has body (6 %), the 17 px date at `#9BAB9C` (0.386) over it is about 4:1. Extend the glass slot to y160-290 (one recess holding date, HH:MM and the status line; rounded ends r 65) so all three sit at 2.7 %; the battery/steps block is on the black earth and needs nothing.
- **F14. Minor.** The "KALI / ORBIT" label at y153 was removed without the user asking; say so or keep it. Zodiac ranges by `[DAY_OF_YEAR]` drift one day in leap years; acceptable, document it.

## Keep (genuinely strong)

- The thesis and the stack: six planes, each a different real clock (seconds/minutes on the flange, apparent solar on the sunken ring, sidereal on the vault, lunar/zodiac objects, civil time on the glass). This is the correct reading of the Galaxia and it satisfies the brief's "at least three clearly separated depth planes, each carrying real time information".
- The projection and the horizon: r = (90 - dec) x 1.875, NCP at centre, Seoul horizon as a fixed curve through (225,295), (56,225)/(394,225), exiting at (23,125)/(427,125). I re-derived the curve at latitude 37.57 and it is right (dec = atan(-cos H / tan 37.57)). A fixed horizon over a turning sky is the one piece of real astronomy on the face that the user can verify by looking up.
- The solar maths: E0 = 1764586194 gives Seoul LST = 0 within about 20 s (I recomputed GMST for 2025-12-01 10:49:54 UTC); sample LST 11h50 verified (GMST 3.37 h + 8.465 h); the EoT (16.2 min on day 298) and the Seoul longitude term (-31.9 deg at the sample time) check; the linearised H0 = 90 + 0.83 dec is within 1 deg of acos(-tan dec tan phi) at both solstices.
- The glass slot under the digits (time as a sunken dial) and the decision to keep HH:MM, baseline, INFINITY, prompt and the 360 ms wake untouched.
- Hairline dragon in AOD: removes the largest static lit mass, cuts the AOD from 11.3 % to about 7 %, and is a legitimate "engraved" reading of the logomark.
- The wake order (deepest plane first, text last) and the explicit WFF Variant fractions; `startOffset`, `interpolation="CUBIC_BEZIER"` with `controls`, and `angleDirection` all exist on Variant.
- Palette discipline: one grisaille, one gold (shared hue family with the existing warning colour), one abyss teal; phosphor reserved for what is alive. Sound, and it survives F2 unchanged.
- The sun pointer as the solar/zodiac instrument and the moon disc as a lunar one are the right complications; the moon could later be placed at its true RA/dec on the vault (studio-computable; WFF approximation from `[MOON_PHASE_POSITION]`), which would make it astronomy instead of a complication.

## Verification log

- Chirality: derived from "stars circle Polaris counter-clockwise" and RA = LST - H; cross-checked with the Plough/Arcturus geometry at LST 12.4 h; single press source for the Galaxia says anticlockwise.
- Dragon extents: flattened the unchanged press-pack path (cubics sampled at 10 steps) in the frame (135,28,180x123): max r 212.9 at (143.6,28.3); 38 of 467 samples beyond r190 between 333.0 and 359.4 deg; top near x225 at y 32.1; perimeter about 1,190 px.
- Luminance: sRGB linearised per channel, Y = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B, for every hex in the proposal (values quoted above).
- WFF: `source-type` reference (`[SECONDS_SINCE_EPOCH]` v3, `[UTC_TIMESTAMP]` ms v1, `[HOUR_0_23_MINUTE]` v1, `[TIMEZONE_OFFSET_MINUTES_DST]` v3, `[MOON_PHASE_TYPE]` 0-7, `[DAY_OF_YEAR]` 1-366); `arithmetic-expression` (trig = Java Math, `rad()`/`deg()`, `%` on int and float, `numberFormat` with `#`/`,`/`.`); Variant (`duration`, `startOffset`, `interpolation`, `controls`, `angleDirection`); PartImage (`angle`, `scaleX/Y`, `alpha`, `pivotX/Y` transformable; one `Variant`, unlimited `Transform`); `Outline` under `Font` (Wear OS 4, `color` required, `width` default 2.0); memory 100 MB active / 10 MB ambient, RGBA8888 = 4 bytes/px after resize-to-screen, ambient counted as up to three full-screen layers; ambient refresh once per minute.
- Not verified (and the proposal must not claim them): on-device filtering of rotated PartImages; any system-level AOD pixel shift for WFF faces; the actual WFF version on this SM-R965N's current firmware.
