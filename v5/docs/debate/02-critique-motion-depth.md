# Critique 02 — "Celestial Vault" (motion & depth lens) vs. the real Galaxia

Reviewer stance: adversarial design director. Everything below was checked against the brief, the v4 source (face.js / celestial.js / tokens.js, preview PNGs), the on-watch WFF XML (declares `com.google.wear.watchface.format.version = 4`), and the WFF reference pages for Variant, Transform, Animation, Arc, Group, PartImage, Font, Outline, source types, arithmetic expressions and memory limits. Numbers marked "computed" were calculated, not taken from the proposal.

## Score: 4 / 10

**Verdict in one line:** right skeleton, dead skin. The proposal is the first one with a genuine multi-plane astronomical stack and the astronomy is correct and buildable, but every "material" surface is specified at 0.1–2 % relative luminance, so none of the depth cues (well shadow, dragon shadow, digit shadow, plate, rim light) can register on an OLED at wrist brightness. Rendered as written, the user sees v4's black print with the same dragon in the same frame, the same digits at the same baseline, plus dust and a dark ring. Not flat on paper; flat on the wrist.

## Answers to the four questions

**Is it STILL one-dimensional?** In structure, no: five z-separated planes with four real time-scales (sidereal vault, solar/lunar pointers, hour rim + seconds on the flange, civil digits) is exactly what the brief asked for. In appearance, yes. Depth on a dial is read through value contrast between levels; Galaxia works because it stacks black lacquer, white inscriptions, light grisaille highlights, gold, and opaline. This proposal's levels, computed as sRGB relative luminance:

| Surface (as specified) | Effective colour | Rel. luminance |
|---|---|---|
| vault base #050A08 → #0A1512 | — | 0.27 % → 0.64 % |
| Milky Way #16302C @55 % over vault | #112420 | 1.5 % |
| constellation lines #2C4A3C @70 % | #223A2F, 0.8 px | 3.6 % |
| well shadow (75 % black on vault) | #020504 | 0.13 % |
| time plate (58 % black on vault) | #040908 | 0.24 % |
| ground (85 % black on vault) | #020303 | 0.08 % |
| flange alloy #1B2A20 → #070B09 | — | 2.0 % → 0.3 % |
| flange rim light #4C6A50 @70 %, 1 px | #3D5742 | 8.2 % |
| dragon shadow (60 % black) on vault | — | lands on a 0.6 % surface |
| digit shadow #99000000 on plate | — | lands on a 0.24 % surface |

A shadow needs a lit surface to fall on. Four of the five occlusion cues in section 1 ("flange→vault, dragon→vault/plate/flange, digits→plate") land on surfaces darker than 1 % luminance: they do not exist visually. The only cross-plane light that survives is the sun's 25 % glow touching the flange. Compare v4: its planet had a visible gradient (#A2B7A2 = 44 % down to #43686C = 12 %). v5 removes the only material object v4 had and replaces it with a sky at 0.3–1.5 %. In value terms the proposal is darker and flatter than v4.

**At least three separated depth planes, each carrying a different real time-scale, with material shading?** Three planes with real time, yes (vault/sidereal, flange/second + hour, digits/civil; sun and moon add solar and lunar). Material shading: specified, not visible — see the table. The 1 px rim light and the 0.8 px lines are below the brief's own threshold ("thinner than ~1 px … is decoration only"; 1 px = 0.089 mm at 40 mm).

**Decoration glued onto the same flat v4 face?** Partly, and more than the author admits. Section 2 keeps v4's layout verbatim: dragon frame (135,24) 180x123 "unchanged", brand y153, date y181, HH/MM at x166/x284 baseline y252, BATTERY/STEPS at x133/x315, gauges, prompt y388 (v4: 389). The brief explicitly allows the dragon's "placement/size/treatment" to change; the proposal does not use that. "It straddles the well rim like the engraved dragons of the JLC Dragon edition": the wing top is at r 201 and the flange inner edge at r 200 — 1 px of overlap is a tangent, not a straddle. The dragon shadow (+2,+4) falls downward onto the 0.6 % vault, not onto the flange. The time plate — a 300x150 black oval at 58 % under the digits — is the "label pasted on a photograph" cliché: it produces a flat hole in the sky exactly where the sky's most interesting region (Polaris, Dipper, Cassiopeia) lives. That is anti-depth.

**Still KALI, or a JLC pastiche?** Still KALI: official dragon, phosphor on everything that moves, prompt + INFINITY, Roboto Mono. But the identity is diluted at the edges: "KALI / VAULT" rename for no reason, 24-h numerals 00/06/18 and zodiac glyphs lifted straight from Galaxia at 9–11 px (pure decoration by the brief's rule), and the terminal's `SEC 24` line replaced by `LST 11:50  SUN SCO  MOON 15D` — a 13 px three-field astronomy dump that is neither terminal nor legible at a glance. Not a pastiche, but the borrowed parts are the least visible ones, so they add clutter without adding Galaxia's feel.

**ONE memorable thing or seven competing ones?** Seven competing, by the proposal's own count ("Seven real time-scales on five planes"). Galaxia's memorable thing is one bright mechanical object travelling the sky. Here the candidate hero, the sun, is a 9 px disc (0.8 mm on the wrist) with a 20 px glow. The moon is 10 px with eight phase bitmaps at 12x12 — the terminator is 1–2 px wide, so the phase is unreadable. The seconds dot is 6.4 px. These three dots orbit on three tracks at r 178, 190 and 207, interleaved with the well edge (196), the minute rim (197.5) and the flange rim light (200.5): six concentric features inside 29 px (2.6 mm). On the 480 px panel these alias into one busy hairline band, and the seconds dot overtakes the sun and the moon every minute. The "signature" the proposal claims is the 360 ms counter-rotation parallax — but a +4° rotation of a 0.6 %-luminance disc is a parallax of nothing; only the dragon's 0.96→1.00 scale (7 px) will be seen.

## FATAL

1. **Value-starved materials (the flatness complaint survives).** All non-text surfaces sit at 0.1–2 % relative luminance (table above). Shadows fall on black; rim lights are 1 px; the "grisaille" peaks at #16302C. Galaxia's grisaille is white enamel on dark; this is dark on darker. The user will look at it and say "still flat".
2. **No hero, crowded rim.** Three 6–10 px orbiting dots and three hairline rings packed into r 178–207. Nothing on the face has the presence of a Cosmotourbillon. The phase moon at 12 px cannot be built legibly at any bitmap quality.
3. **Composition is v4's.** Dragon frame, every text baseline and the telemetry grid are unchanged; the stack was inserted around the old print. The dragon neither straddles the rim (1 px) nor casts a visible shadow. The black oval plate under the digits flattens the one area where the sky could have been seen.
4. **Not a hard-constraint break, but a credibility break: the AOD lit estimate.** v4's AOD measured 11.3 % non-zero pixels with a sparser content set (solid dragon, outline digits, 4 arcs, 12 ticks, BAT line, INFINITY). v5's AOD adds date, two 27 px values, the full prompt line, 24 stars, two rings and keeps the dragon silhouette (1.2 px stroke + 18 % fill covers the same pixels as a solid fill), yet claims 9.9 %. The estimate ignores anti-aliasing and cannot be lower than v4's; expect 13–15 %, i.e. at the ceiling. "Quiet AOD" — a prior decision the user approved — is being traded for seven lit text items.

## FIXES (required before this can be built)

1. **Re-grade the stack into three visible value bands and verify with a histogram.** Targets (relative luminance): ground ≈ 0.1 % (true black lacquer), vault 3–5 % (base #0E1C17 → #16302A; Milky Way wash up to #2E5A4C @70 %; lines 1.2 px #4F7A5E @85 %; stars V≤3 at 2.5–4 px with 8 px halos), flange 5–8 % (#3A4C41 top → #141C17 bottom, 2 px rim light #8FA894 upper-left, ticks #6F8C73), digits/dragon 70–90 %. Only then does the well shadow (vault → #04080A over r 180→196) and the dragon shadow read. Acceptance test in the studio: luminance histogram of the active frame shows at least three separated peaks besides black and the text.
2. **One hero: make the sun the Cosmotourbillon.** Disc r 7 at radius 196 (on the lip, not inside it), 2 px #FFE2A8 upper-left highlight, a baked 64x64 glow bitmap rotating in the same Group (≈25 % at 12 px → 0 at 32 px) so it visibly lights both the flange alloy and the vault edge as it travels. Reduce the seconds to a single 4 px dot at r 212, no trail (the hour rim already gives a growing arc). Move the moon into the sky where it belongs: a 20 px body at radius 160 inside the lens with 20x20 phase PNGs, or drop the body and keep the moon in the readout only. Result: two orbit tracks (196, 212) instead of four.
3. **Use the permission the brief gives: let the dragon actually wrap over the rim.** Frame (125,8) 200x137 (wing tips at r ≈ 214, overlapping the flange by ~14 px) with the shadow (+2,+4, 60 %) falling on the lit alloy, where it is visible. Keep the official path untouched.
4. **Delete the 58 % black plate.** Separate the digits from the sky the way JLC separates inscriptions from the vault: no plate. Use the WFF text decorations that the proposal wrongly says do not exist — `Font` accepts `<Outline color width>`, `<Shadow>` and `<OutGlow>` children (Outline: colour required, width default 2.0, transformable colour from v4). A 2 px #000 @70 % Outline plus Shadow on the 78/600 TimeText keeps <1 s legibility over 2.6 px stars without a hole in the sky; Polaris becomes a visible 3 px star in the colon gap instead of a conceit.
5. **Merge the rim hairlines.** The well edge (196), the minute rim (197.5) and the flange rim light (200.5) become ONE lit lip: static rim light at 20 %, overdrawn by the phosphor minute Arc at 100 % from 12 o'clock to the current minute ("the lip lights up around the hour"). Remove the separate r 197.5 arc.
6. **Fix R5 the right way.** AOD digits: Font color #11200F + `<Outline color="#ff91AD80" width="1.4">`. Delete the "10 pre-rendered outline glyph PNGs selected by Condition" fallback — it would be 40 PNGs and is unnecessary.
7. **AOD diet and measurement.** Drop the prompt prefix and the STEPS value in AOD (keep date, BAT value, INFINITY); measure lit % with the same 900→450 raster method used for v4; target ≤ 12 %. Luminance-weighted figures are not the brief's metric.
8. **Horizon hairline under the digits.** Computed: the horizon circle passes (120, 257.7) and (330, 257.7), 5–6 px under the outer corners of HH and MM (baseline 252). Mask the hairline for x 110–340 so no curved line slices under the numerals.
9. **Prompt position.** At baseline y388 the prompt's ends reach r 184–191 (computed), violating the proposal's own safe radius (176) and sitting inside its own well-shadow annulus (186–200), so the "inscription on the ground" is darkened at both ends. Baseline y380 keeps the descenders inside r 181 and still inside the well chord (±120 px at that y).
10. **Readout.** One line, ≤ 18 characters, prompt style: `sun sco · moon 15d`. Drop LST from the wrist glance (it is for the appendix, not the face). 13 px minimum.
11. **Animation frame rate.** WFF `Animation` defaults to `fps="15"`; the 180 ms ease-out seconds step would be 2–3 frames. Set `fps="60"` on both the dot and (if kept) the trail.
12. **Regenerate Appendix A from the studio catalogue, not by hand.** Computed for 2026-10-25 10:08:24 KST: DINT 9794, DFRAC −0.4525 (not −0.4528), GMST 50.5°, LST 177.5° = 11h50m, VAULT_ANGLE 182.5° — the proposal's numbers are right. But Cassiopeia (RA ≈ 1h) has hour angle +162.5° at that LST: it is low in the NNW approaching lower culmination, not "low NE". The sample state was guessed, which means the star map was never rendered against the proposed sidereal expression. Make the studio draw it.
13. **Memory estimate.** 3.2 MB counts only the bitmaps. The evaluator assumes up to three full-screen ambient layers (3 × 450×450×4 ≈ 2.4 MB) plus the vector font; realistic ambient total ≈ 6.5 MB. Still under 10 MB, but run the google/watchface memory footprint evaluator before claiming it.

## KEEP (genuinely strong — do not lose these in the rework)

- **The well architecture and the occlusion chain.** vault < ground/horizon < rim light < flange < sun/moon < seconds < dragon < digits is the right z-order, and the reasoning "every plane leaves a mark on the plane beneath it" is exactly how Galaxia reads. Only the values are wrong, not the stack.
- **The terminal on the ground below Seoul's horizon.** The best single idea in the proposal: the sky is real, the earth is the black lacquer, the inscriptions are on the earth. Keep the Seoul-fixed horizon circle — computed, centre (225,79.7) R 206.7 matches the true azimuthal-equidistant horizon for 37.57 N to within ~1 px at the N, NE (A=45°) and E points, and the ground/well intersection at y 167 is correct.
- **The astronomy, verified:** the DINT/DFRAC split of GMST is correct (360·DINT ≡ 0 mod 360, so 0.98564736629·DINT is exact); `VAULT_ANGLE = (720 − LST) % 360` with RA increasing clockwise and CCW rotation is self-consistent with the real northern sky (east on the right, culmination at the meridian index); the sun pointer and the ecliptic point share a radial line by construction, so the zodiac reading needs no second ring; the moon at `SUN_ANGLE + age·12.19°` culminates at 18:00 at first quarter and opposes the sun at full; mean noon 12:32 KST and EOT ≈ +16 min on 25 Oct are right; moon age ≈ 15 d on 25 Oct 2026 is right.
- **The WFF mapping is buildable, checked against the v4 reference:** `angle`, `alpha`, `scaleX/Y`, `tintColor` (v4) are transformable on Group and PartImage, so `Variant` may target them; `Arc startAngle/endAngle` are transformable; `Animation` carries `duration`, `interpolation` (incl. `CUBIC_BEZIER` + `controls`), `angleDirection`; `Variant` has `startOffset`, `duration` (animates from v4), `interpolation`, `controls`; `[HOUR_0_23_MINUTE]` (float, v1), `[DAY_OF_YEAR]`, `[YEAR]`, `[TIMEZONE_OFFSET_MINUTES_DST]` (integer minutes, v3), `[MOON_PHASE_POSITION]` (0–28 days, v1), `[MOON_PHASE_TYPE]` (0–7, v1), the HOUR/MINUTE digit sources (v2), `sin`, `rad`, `floor`, `clamp`, `subText`, `numberFormat` and float `%` all exist. Numeric precision is unspecified in the docs, so the small-number formulation is the right precaution.
- **The nested-Group rule** (a Variant and a live Transform never target the same attribute on the same element).
- **The wake order** deep → shallow, 360 ms, and "it goes dark, it does not go away" for Active → AOD; the dragon scale 0.96 → 1.00 is the one wake move that will actually be seen.
- **Seconds as an escapement tick, not a sweep.** Discrete, terminal-like, frame-cheap.
- **Palette discipline:** phosphor only on things that move or are KALI, gold only on the sun, teal only on the Milky Way, no fourth hue.
- **Polaris behind the colon** — keep the idea, make it visible (fix 4).

## Verification notes

- WFF version on the device build: `KALIWATCH/wff/watchface/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml` declares format version 4; all elements cited above are available at or below v4.
- Font decorations: the Font reference lists `Shadow`, `Outline`, `OutGlow`, `Underline`, `StrikeThrough` as inner elements; the Outline reference gives `color` (required) and `width` (float, default 2.0). The proposal's "WFF Font has no stroke" is false.
- Memory: limits are 10 MB ambient / 100 MB interactive; the footprint method decompresses bitmaps, crops transparent pixels, may downsample to RGB565 / ALPHA_8, and assumes up to three full-screen layers in ambient.
- Geometry (computed): sky lens area ≈ 70,070 px² of the 120,687 px² well; the dragon frame (22,140 px²) and the plate (35,343 px²) cover most of it, leaving two side crescents ≈ 90 px wide as the only unoccluded sky; equator at r 147, ecliptic between r 109 and r 185, zenith at (225,139), north horizon at (225,286).
- Physical scale: 1 px = 0.089 mm at 40 mm; sun 0.8 mm, moon 0.9 mm, flange 2.2 mm.
- Not verified (left as the proposal's risks): whether Samsung's One UI Watch plays WFF ambient-transition Variants at a perceptible duration (R6), bilinear shimmer of 1.2 px stars under bitmap rotation (R2), and actual AOD luminance on the panel (R10).
