# Critique 02 — "Celestial Vault" (horologist lens), depth audit

**Score: 5 / 10. Verdict: right skeleton, wrong skin.** The spec describes a five-level section with real time on every level; the pixels it specifies describe a black disc with dots, white digits, a green dragon and a thin, over-worked rim. Every depth cue the proposal relies on is placed on a surface whose luminance is between 0 % and 1.1 %, so on the Galaxy Watch6 Classic OLED in any light brighter than a dim room the "stack" collapses back into v4's print. The astronomy and the WFF mapping are the strongest work this project has produced; the picture is not yet built on them.

## The four questions, answered

**1. Is it still one-dimensional?** Visually, yes — about 1.5 planes. Conceptually, no. The proposal has the *architecture* of depth (vault / rings / digits / cage) but not the *value structure* of depth. Measured in relative luminance (sRGB → linear): lacquer bed #000 = 0 %; moat = 0 %; hour-ring floor #070B08 = 0.30 %; vault base #050B0E = 0.30 %, falling to 0.05 % at the limb after the 0.15 shade; flange satin #141C17 = 1.1 %. Five "physical levels" within 1.1 % of each other. At a 500-nit peak that is 0–5.5 nits. What survives is what v4 already had: bright things on black (stars, indexes, arc, digits, dragon), plus a 1.5 px rim-light hairline on the upper-left limb. That is an outline of a dome, not a dome.

**2. Three separated planes, each with a real time scale, with material shading?** Time scales: genuinely yes, and this is the proposal's real achievement — sidereal vault, 24 h sun, minute arc, civil digits, lunation porthole, year on the zodiac band. Separation: no, because in a top-down orthographic view the only separation cues available are value contrast, cast shadow and occlusion, and the proposal spends all three on near-black grounds. Material shading: specified, bakeable, but there is no *chiaro* in the chiaroscuro. The one object in v4 that had actual mid-tone material (the planet, #43686C mid, #A2B7A2 light) was retired and nothing mid-tone replaced it.

**3. KALI or JLC pastiche?** Pastiche with a Kali sticker. Every *new* surface is JLC vocabulary transplanted part-for-part: opaline flange, soleil-guilloche hour ring, applied rhodium batons with bevel highlights, gold sun, grisaille sky, porthole moon, dauphine-era "applied numerals". Everything KALI is inherited from v4 (dragon, phosphor arc, mono digits, prompt). The word KALI has left the face — "KALI / ORBIT" at y 153 was replaced by `LST 11:50 · SUN IN SCO`; the brand now exists only inside a 13 px `kali@vault:~$`. Phosphor is reduced to hairlines (1.5 px arc, 20 px cage ring, colon, gauge fills). The brief says the owner's background is radar signal processing; concentric range rings and a slow sweep are native to both PPI displays and the terminal, and the proposal did not use that lens once.

**4. One memorable thing or seven?** Seven. The proposal never names its hero. The 22 px rim alone carries four instruments (minute arc, 12 indexes, 24 solar ticks, orbiting cage). The vault carries 130 stars, 20 figures, 6 names, a Milky Way, an ecliptic, a 12-sector band with 12 names. The lower block carries two gauges, a porthole, five labels and a prompt. The candidate hero — the Kali dragon perched on a turning real sky — is buried, and the sky's turning is invisible because the sky is black.

## FATAL (would still read as flat, or breaks the brief)

**F1 — No dynamic range for the shading.** All shadows fall on 0–0.3 % L grounds: the digit shadow (#000 at 65 % over #050B0E → (2,4,5)) is a 3-code difference nobody can see; the dome shade darkens 0.3 % to 0.05 %; the "engraving shadow" #1E3A17 at 60 % over #050B0E composites to ≈ (20,39,19), which is *brighter* than the dome it falls on — it is a dim green drop-glow, not a shadow. The proposal's own Risk 2 ("OLED black crush") is not a risk; it is the default state of the design.

**F2 — The "three real-time occlusions" are one.** (a) The cage is an unfilled 1.5 px ring: it covers nothing when it crosses an index; (b) "the sun dimming when it enters the dome's rim shadow" has no mechanism anywhere in the spec — z4 has no alpha or tint expression, and the 50 % dim is baked into the bed's hairline, not applied to the sun; (c) only the moon porthole actually occludes. Section 10's depth argument ("occlusion is the depth cue the brain trusts most") stands on claims the layer table does not implement.

**F3 — The physical stack is 2 mm wide and the flying object is a dot.** The whole Galaxia translation lives in r 200–225: 22 design px, 11 % of the radius, ≈ 2 mm at 40 mm. The reference's flanges plus hour ring take roughly a fifth of its radius and its Cosmotourbillon, by eye in the press photographs, is about a fifth of the dial diameter (≈ 90 px in this space). The proposal's cage is Ø20 with a 1 × 8 px seconds hand (0.7 mm): not a flying tourbillon, a rim bead. Worse, the sun (24 h) and the cage (23 h 56 m) sit on the same rim 8 px apart moving at rates that differ by 0.99°/day — to the eye they are two beads on one clock, which erases the very sidereal/solar distinction that justifies the cage. The press text says the dome "wraps around the Cosmotourbillon", i.e. the cage travels *inside* the dome structure, not across the flanges. Also: the existing on-watch XML keeps its outermost ring at r 216–217; nothing in this project has verified that r 218–225 is visible under the Classic's rotating bezel.

**F4 — Identity requirement fails.** The brief's definition of success includes "must still read as KALI, not as a JLC clone". See question 3: brand word removed, all new material is JLC-literal, phosphor demoted to hairlines, three new hues on the dominant surfaces.

## FIXES (required, in priority order)

1. **Give the vault a real grisaille range.** Base #1C2A30 (2.1 % L), lit zenith zone #35484F (6.0 % L), limb #070D10; bed stays #000 so the dome stands proud of a black moat. That is a 20:1 range inside the dome that an OLED can show, stars read as white enamel on dark grey as on the reference, and digits stay ≥ 11:1 on the lightest zone. The brief explicitly permits grisaille greys; terminal black survives as the bed, the moat and the below-horizon lens (fix 2).
2. **Centre the shading on the zenith and bake the horizon.** For Seoul (37.57 N) on this chart the zenith is fixed at (225, 137.6) — 87 px above the pole — not at an arbitrary "apex" (190,185). Make (225,138) the brightest point. The real horizon passes through (225, 287.6) at 6 o'clock and leaves the r 200 limb at ±63.6° from 12 o'clock (≈ 2 and 10 o'clock). Everything inside that curve is the sky actually above Seoul now; the lens below it is under the horizon and can be the darkest material — and it is exactly where the sub-dial row (y 298–366) sits. Result: dragon on the zenith, time on the pole, telemetry below the horizon; three planes each with a reason, and the claim "the vault behind 10:08 is the Seoul sky at that instant" becomes true (today it is a planisphere that also shows stars below the horizon). Cost: zero — the shade PNG is static.
3. **Re-proportion the rim and move the cage inside the dome.** Keep the vault at r 200, but the rim carries ONE scale: 12 indexes + 60 ticks + the minute arc. Delete the 24-tick solar hairline and the soleil texture; the sun's position is self-explanatory with noon at 12. Put the cage at r 178–188 (orbit radius 183, Ø36–40), inside the limb as on the reference, phosphor ring 2 px, seconds hand 1.5 × 14 px, hub Ø4, **with a fill** (#0B1E26 at 90 %) so it genuinely occludes stars and the zodiac band as it travels. That is the one bright slow object the brief asked for, and the one true occlusion.
4. **Implement the sun's rim-shadow entry or delete the claim.** WFF: `<Transform target="tintColor" value="(SUN > 90 && SUN < 200) ? colorRgb(138,100,36) : colorRgb(227,179,94)"/>` on the sun PartImage (tintColor is transformable from WFF v4 — verified on the Group, PartImage and PartDraw pages), or an alpha Transform 255 → 150 across the same sector.
5. **Honour the time-scale ladder in the geometry.** §10 claims "nearer = faster", §3 puts minutes at −3 (deepest), civil digits at +2, the 24 h sun at 0. Move the minute arc to r 199 so it rides the dome's rim light (above the vault), keep the indexes on the bed. New order, near to far: cage (s) > digits (civil) > minute arc on the limb (min) > sun (24 h) > vault (sidereal) > zodiac band (year) > moon in its porthole (lunation). Redraw the section diagram to match.
6. **Restore the identity.** (a) `KALI / VAULT` 12 px, letter-spacing 3, phosphor at y 160; move `LST 11:50 · SUN IN SCO` to the deepest level as a transferred inscription in the below-horizon lens (x 225, baseline 374, 11 px #9BAB9C 50 %) — on the reference, inscriptions live on the base disc, not beside the dial name. (b) Draw indexes and ticks as range-ring glyphs — 1 px phosphor at 20 % with the four cardinals 2 × 6 px #A6F46A — not bevelled rhodium batons. (c) Keep gold for the sun only and grisaille for the sky only; everything else phosphor/paper. Retire the #C2CFCB indexes and the #2A3A2D lip highlights.
7. **Protect the official dragon from the star field.** Bake a 25 % black soft ellipse at (225,92) rx 100 ry 70 into the shade PNG, and make the engraving shadow actually dark: #000 at 60 %, offset (+1.5,+2). Stars poking through the logomark's thin strokes is brand damage.
8. **Restore telemetry hierarchy.** Values back to 27 px (baseline 343), gauges to r 18, as the proposal's own Risk 11 offers; the moon becomes a Ø30 aperture at (225,330) between them. The brief's hard line: time/date/battery/steps are the information; a moon is instrument.
9. **Cut the AOD inventory back to v4's seven groups.** Keep: outline digits, dim dragon, date, 40 drifting stars, sun ring, cage ring, minute arc (all drift or are green-dominant and ≤ 25 % L). Drop: 24 solar ticks, the three static sub-dial outlines at 30 %, the 30 % moon frame; show `BAT 78%` as v4 did. Lit-pixel % passing is not the same as quiet.
10. **Specify chart chirality and add a studio test.** For `SKY = 360 − LST` to be correct, the sky bitmap must be drawn as seen from inside (RA increasing **clockwise** from the 12 o'clock reference at zero rotation). A catalogue plotted as a map (RA anticlockwise) runs the sky backwards with no visible error for weeks. Test: at the sample moment (LST 177.5°) Regulus (RA 152.1°, dec +11.97°) must render at r 130.1, cw angle 334.6°, i.e. (169, 108).
11. **Bake zodiac sector ticks at the RA of each 30° ecliptic boundary**, not every 30°: 0, 27.9, 57.8, 90, 122.2, 152.1, 180, 207.9, 237.8, 270, 302.2, 332.1 deg. Otherwise the band disagrees with the `[DAY_OF_YEAR]` text for ≈ 2 days at the 60/120/240/300° boundaries.
12. **State the mean-sun error.** The pointer reads the *mean* sun; the true sun's RA differs by the equation of time (≈ +16 min on 2026-10-25: true RA 13h58m vs the proposal's 14h14m). Fine for a 30° band; say so in the docs, or add `9.87·sin(2B) − 7.53·cos(B) − 1.5·sin(B)` minutes with `B = 360·([DAY_OF_YEAR] − 81)/365` to the studio and the WFF `SUN` expression.
13. **WFF corrections.** (a) Drop the twin-image cross-fades for dragon and sun: `tintColor` is transformable from v4, so a `Variant target="tintColor"` is legal per the Variant spec ("must be a transformable attribute") — saves three bitmaps. (b) "Per-second redraw limited to a 20 × 20 group" is unsupported: WFF documents no dirty-region rendering; budget a full composite per second in Active, including the rotated 400 px vault, and measure. (c) `TimeText format="hh"` alone is not among the documented examples (`hh_10`, `hh_1`, `m`, `ss`, `h:mm` are); verify or use the `_10/_1` pairs. (d) `controls` for `CUBIC_BEZIER` is documented as a 4-component vector `<0.22, 1, 0.36, 1>`; confirm the literal the validator accepts. (e) The ambient memory figure of 3.3 MB is not how the validator counts ("assumes ambient mode uses up to three full-screen layers, two of which are static"); run the validator rather than quoting a sum.

## KEEP (genuinely strong)

- **The astronomy — it is correct.** Recomputed independently for 2026-10-25 10:08:24 KST: D = 9793.548, LST = 177.5° (11h50m), SKY = 182.5°, SUN = 35.9°, sun in SCO — all within 0.1° of the proposal; the 0.5347 h mean-time offset for 126.98 E is right; the float32 ulp at 3.5 × 10⁶ is 0.25° as claimed; the leap-year day count is exact for 2001–2099.
- **The WFF mapping is mostly real.** Verified against the current reference: `[HOUR_0_23_MINUTE]` (float, v1), `[TIMEZONE_OFFSET_MINUTES_DST]` (integer, v3), `[MOON_PHASE_POSITION]` 0–28 inclusive (29 frames correct), `[STEP_GOAL]`, `[DAY_OF_YEAR]` 1–366, `[YEAR]` all exist as named; `%` works on floats, `floor`/`clamp` exist; Arc `startAngle`/`endAngle` are transformable and Arc takes `Transform`; Variant `duration`/`startOffset` are 0.0–1.0 fractions, `CUBIC_BEZIER` with `controls` exists; BitmapFont is referenced by family name from TimeText; limits are 10 MB ambient / 100 MB interactive.
- **Polaris under the colon, civil clockwise / celestial anticlockwise.** The one move that is both Galaxia-true and KALI-true: the time sits on the pole. Keep it as the organising idea.
- **One light at 10:30 and the recess logic** (shadow on the upper-left inner wall, lip light lower-right) — correct, keep once the grounds have range to show it.
- **Back-to-front wake order**, parallax kick on the deepest layer only, nothing translating the time, 360 ms, reduced motion instant.
- **Palette discipline:** gold only for the solar system of time, grisaille only through alpha, never on UI text.
- **Honesty:** planets not faked; hardcoded Seoul longitude declared; `MOON_PHASE_POSITION` as a mechanical daily step.
- **The porthole mechanism** (bezel image over phase image) — the one real occlusion; keep it, move it.
- **Dragon path and HH:MM metrics unchanged.**
